# Known limitations — vertical slice

- District art is original procedural geometry; citizens and vehicles use detailed CC0 models. This is not a photorealistic recreation of Monrovia. The district and travel distances are intentionally fictionalized/compressed.
- The showroom's integrated purchase/garage flow and desktop/mobile layouts were checked in headless Chromium with software WebGL. Hardware-GPU frame rates, broad browser/device coverage and accessibility audits remain unmeasured. Existing vehicles have no modeled interiors or opening doors, so those controls are omitted. Paint/finish is visual-only; the two original modern procedural models remain stylized rather than photorealistic.
- Automated asset/gameplay checks do not replace browser visual review. Target-GPU visual quality, animation blending, responsive phone layout and performance remain release gates. Headlight rigs and replicated state are tested, but beam appearance and day/night readability still require browser/GPU review. Home layout/collision/light logic is tested, but room composition, camera clearance, furniture materials and compact-phone visual quality likewise require browser review.
- Local persistence uses one Node process and SQLite. PostgreSQL/Redis are documented scaling steps, not running services.
- Multiplayer is a small-session baseline. No thousands-player or regional handoff claim has been load-tested.
- Client-predicted movement has plausibility checks, not full server physics or a comprehensive anti-cheat product. Solid actor/prop collision is discrete 2D client simulation without rigid-body momentum or continuous network arbitration; ambient signal obedience is also client-side. Civic speed enforcement uses accepted displacement history inside two marked zones, not exhaustive road-rule coverage.
- Player-to-player health is server-issued from accepted movement and owns recovery/respawn timers, but it is session-scoped rather than a persistent casualty/medical system. Ambient NPC injury and recovery are local per-client state. Ambient traffic is not server-simulated; no general server rigid-body contact, ambient-versus-player injury arbitration, continuous collision physics or cross-process health consistency is claimed.
- Civic departments, personnel, service rules and penalties are fictional. Worker/officer routines are deterministic ambient game AI; there are no real agency integrations, punitive arrests, pursuits, weapons or courts.
- Taxi jobs are NPC-only. Human passenger matchmaking, ratings, disputes, schedules and shared rides are not yet implemented.
- The implemented lifestyle slice has two fixed home listings, five vehicle listings (including two original modern procedural models) and three upgrade types. Player-to-player markets, rentals, businesses, port/airport operations, boats, motorcycles, custom floorplans/multi-unit housing and custom vehicle inventories remain roadmap systems.
- Phone calls are local scripted NPC text interactions. They do not connect to telecom networks, real people, generative agents or player voice chat.
- Fuel/condition are displayed from persistent vehicle records and change during local driving, but the complete refuel/repair economy and cross-session driving-wear reconciliation are not yet available.
- Audio is synthesized locally. Patrol siren nodes, gains and visual lightbars were browser-checked, but no human listening or device-speaker verification is claimed. No voice chat is claimed. Music and recorded regional ambience await licensed production assets.
- Desktop keyboard/mouse is primary. Basic gamepad and touch controls exist, but screen-reader, remapping, mobile performance and broad device accessibility audits remain.
- Backup/restore, security review and multi-client soak tests must be completed before a public release.
